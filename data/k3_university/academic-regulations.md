# Academic Regulations for Full-Time Undergraduate Programs

## Glossary of Terms

- **MOET:** Ministry of Education and Training.
- **University:** VinUniversity/VinUni.
- **College:** an academic unit that undergraduate students are registered in academic programs.
- **Dean:** Head of a College or a Faculty.
- **Program Director:** academic staff member responsible for curriculum development and assurance of learning for majors/minors in a program.
- **Course Leader:** academic staff member responsible for a specific course or group of courses.
- **Course Instructor:** academic staff responsible for delivering a specific course.
- **Degree:** e.g., Bachelor of Science in Electrical Engineering, Bachelor of Business Administration, Bachelor of Nursing.
- **Major (Concentration):** the core competence area comprising a structured group of courses in an academic discipline.
- **Minor:** a structured group of courses focused on a particular discipline for added depth.
- **Double Degree:** two degrees earned by fulfilling all requirements of both programs.
- **Credit Transfer:** agreed, consistent credit outcomes based on equivalence between matched academic programs.
- **Full-time student:** undertaking at least 80% of a full-time load (normal annual full-time load is 30 credits).
- **Conferral:** the date an academic qualification is granted (appears on the official parchment).
- **Academic Standing:** an indicator of students' academic progress.

## Chapter I. General Provision

### Article 1. Governing Scope and Regulated Entities

This document governs educational arrangements, examinations, assessment, grading, and graduation requirements based on an academic credit system for full-time undergraduate students at VinUniversity, and also applies to exchange and visiting students.

### Article 2. Medium of Instruction and Assessment

English is the medium of instruction unless otherwise approved for a specific course or program. The College of Arts and Sciences may teach MOET-required courses in Vietnamese (materials provided in English on request). The College of Health Sciences (CHS) may conduct courses/clinical rotations in Vietnamese or bilingually; international students must demonstrate Vietnamese proficiency at level B2 before entering the Medical Doctor Program in Year 4 (Cohort 2025 onward; earlier cohorts encouraged). Clinical rotations occur in the second half of undergraduate programs, where official documents and facility communication are in Vietnamese. National practice licensing exams for medical/nursing graduates are conducted in Vietnamese. See the Vietnamese Language Program Guidelines For International Students for support details.

### Article 3. Academic Program and Syllabi

A program involves degree levels, learning outcomes, required credits, an academic plan, teaching methodology, assessment mode, and other implementation conditions, complying with MOET requirements and University approval. Each program is affiliated with one or more majors/concentrations/minors, typically including General Education, College/Faculty requirements, major requirements, and free electives.

### Article 4. Course and Credit

A credit equals 50 hours of student study (contact hours, tutorials, self-study, experiential learning, assessments, exams): roughly 15 contact hours for lecture/classroom courses, 30–45 hours for practicum/lab courses, 45–90 hours for fieldwork-based courses, 45–60 hours for independent studies (thesis/internship). Students should spend at least 30 self-study hours per credit. Non-credit-bearing courses (e.g., internship/practicum, service-learning) do not carry credit.

### Article 5. Class Meeting Times

Classes run 8:00 a.m.–5:30 p.m. Monday–Friday; each period lasts 50 or 75 minutes (other durations need Provost approval); consecutive classes have 10–15 minute intervals. Evening/weekend sessions can run until 9:25 p.m. with Dean and Registrar permission. Short-period courses are capped at 15 hours/week and 4 hours/day. Instructors must maintain "Open Office Hours" at least equal to the credit hours of their highest-credit course; these may be held online.

### Article 6. Student Academic Performance Evaluation

Evaluated each semester via: credits earned, Semester GPA (SGPA, 4-point scale, total grade points ÷ attempted credits in the semester), cumulative academic load (total credits attempted with passing grades D- and above since program start), and Cumulative GPA (CGPA, total grade points ÷ total attempted credits to date).

## Chapter II. Educational Arrangements

### Article 7. Academic Planning and Duration of Study

Curriculum duration: minimum 180 credits (six-year program) or 120 credits (four-year program). An academic year has Fall and Spring semesters, each with 15 weeks of study, 1 independent study/revision week, and 2 weeks of final exams (medical programs may be exempted from the independent week, case-by-case, by the Dean with the Registrar). Pre-Fall and Summer sessions run alongside main semesters. Maximum time to earn a degree: planned duration plus 4 semesters (four-year program) or plus 6 semesters (five-to-six-year program); the Provost can extend this for unusual circumstances. A double degree's duration must not exceed the maximum duration of the first degree.

### Article 8. Program Enrollment

Admitted students provide a full enrollment package per the Undergraduate Admissions Regulations; the Provost signs the recognition decision. Students with an undeclared major must declare their home major by the end of year two. Major changes need Dean approval, effective the following semester. Full-time students may not enroll full/part-time in another award at VinUni or elsewhere unless approved.

### Article 9. Class Arrangements

Minimum class size is generally 10 students (Provost approval needed for exceptions). Classes below the minimum may be cancelled, with affected students reassigned or required to adjust their registration to meet the minimum semester study load.

### Article 10. Course Registration

The Office of Registrar announces the tentative academic calendar, required/elective course lists, descriptions, prerequisites, and exam formats at the start of each year. Registration must comply with prerequisites and program requirements; exceptions need Program Director approval.

### Article 11. Study Load

Normal full-time load is 30 credits/year; full-time status requires at least 80% of a normal load per regular semester (≥12 credits).

| Variation | Academic load per regular semester | Remarks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normal load | At least 14 credits (first-semester students or good-standing-or-higher students; not applicable in the final semester); 10–14 credits for students with poor prior-semester standing | Completes degree requirements within the normal duration |
| Automatic Overload | 18–22 credits, reviewed by Academic Advisor/Program Director | Allows pace matching student needs/capability |
| Beyond automatic overload | More than 22 credits, requires College Dean approval | |

Summer session: up to 9 credits, no required minimum. Overload petitions are required for first-semester first-years requesting >18 credits, students not in good standing requesting >18 credits, or anyone requesting >22 credits in a regular semester. Prerequisite exemptions require Program Director approval.

### Article 12. Course Add, Drop, and Withdrawal

- **Add:** no later than the close of business day 10 of a regular semester (day 5 for mini semesters).
- **Drop:** no later than business day 15 of a regular semester (day 10 for Summer); dropped courses are not recorded on the academic record.
- **Withdrawal ("W" grade):** after the add/drop period, with no grade penalty, before completing more than 30% of the course's study time; withdrawals are capped at 18 credits over the entire program. Adding a course after add/drop ends requires academic advisor/Registrar approval and instructor discretion.

### Article 13. Transfer of Credit and Course Exemption

Credit transfer may be granted for studies completed elsewhere (international/local exchange, summer programs, other external study), recorded with a "T – Transfer" grade that normally does not count toward GPA. Maximum transferable load is 50% of the program's minimum academic load. Course exemption (no credit transfer) is recorded with a "Z – Exemption" grade and can satisfy prerequisite requirements. Applications: before admission (disclosed in the application, submitted before the first semester) or during enrollment (submitted within 1 month of returning to VinUni study, evaluated by the Office of Registrar). See also the Procedural Guidelines for Credit Transfer Requests.

### Article 14. Re-study/Improvement Study

Retaking a course is the student's own responsibility. Failed required courses must be retaken (or an equivalent/alternative course); failed electives require an equivalent elective. Students may retake a passed course to improve CGPA, but only one repeat attempt is allowed after the first attempt; only the most recent grade counts toward GPA.

### Article 15. Leave of Absence or Sick Leave

Voluntary leave/suspension is requested from the College Dean for 1–2 semesters (extendable). Grounds include military service, official international competition representation, medical emergency/serious illness (certified, translated/notarized if needed), or personal/family hardship (minimum one semester of prior study required; counts toward official study duration). Applications go through the academic advisor and Office of Registrar; return applications must be submitted at least 1 week before the new semester with supporting documentation, or the student's record becomes inactive and reapplication through Admissions is required. The University may impose an involuntary leave of absence in extraordinary circumstances, as a last resort after encouraging a voluntary leave.

### Article 17. Academic Warning, Probation and Dismissal

| Level | Criteria |
| --- | --- |
| Academic Warning | CGPA below 1.20 (freshman), 1.40 (sophomore), 1.60 (junior), or 1.80 (senior+); or semester GPA below 0.80 (first semester)/1.00 (later); or failed credits exceed half the semester's registered credits, or total failed credits exceed 24 since program start |
| Academic Probation | Two consecutive Academic Warnings; requires academic-advisor approval to register courses; remains until CGPA reaches 1.40/1.60/1.80 (sophomore/junior/senior+) |
| Academic Dismissal | Study time exceeds the maximum allowed, or three total Academic Warnings (probation counts as two); recommended by the Dean, decided by the Provost; students retain accumulated academic results |

### Article 18. Academic Honors

**Dean's List:** minimum SGPA 3.60 (at least 12 letter-graded credits), no failed course, and no recorded major misconduct in that semester. Medical students Years 4–6 are evaluated annually (Fall+Spring CGPA) instead of by the traditional semester process due to clinical scheduling.

### Article 19. Double Degree

Combines two degrees from different disciplines (same or different Colleges). Eligibility: registration only from year two of the first program onward, CGPA ≥2.5, meeting the second program's entry threshold, and no active discipline at warning level or higher; must register at least 2 years before the second program's tentative graduation. Falling below good standing in the first program requires withdrawal from the second program starting the next semester. Registration process (5 steps): application to the Registrar Office → eligibility evaluation → Program Director course-equivalence review → Review Board (≥3 members) approval → Provost decision, notified at least 3 weeks before the semester. Second-program tuition follows that program's financial regulations at the standard per-credit rate for additional credits. Graduation from the second program requires meeting first-program graduation requirements too.

### Article 20. Program Change and Institutional Transfer

**Internal transfer (major/degree/College change):** requires meeting admission requirements for the new program, not being a first-year or final-year student (for degree/College changes), and no pending suspension; limited to one change per academic year, effective the following semester.

**External transfer:** permitted to universities with matching disciplines, with approval from both Provosts, except for students who don't meet the receiving university's admission requirements, are freshmen/final-year students, or are disciplined at warning level or higher. Procedure: full document package to the Office of the Registrar → Registrar/Program Director/Dean recommendation to the Provost → Provost decision conditional on the receiving university's acceptance.

## Chapter III. Examination and Course Assessment

### Article 21. Course Assessment, Class Attendance, and Absence

Assessment plans (methods, tasks, weights) are fixed in the syllabus before class and should not change during the semester (except unforeseen circumstances); online assessment contributes no more than 50% of the total weighted grade. Attendance is each student's responsibility per the course syllabus; unexcused absence yields no accommodation. Planned absences require advance instructor notice and justification (e.g., conference presentation, official University representation); unplanned/emergency absences (illness, family emergency, hazardous weather, etc.) require prompt notice to the instructor or, if unreachable, the Office of the Registrar. Make-up plans are at the instructor's discretion, considering feasibility and advance notice.

**Absence from examinations:** valid reasons include overlapping exams within 24 hours, medical emergencies (with documentation), verified family emergencies, or approved University activities (documented at least 1 week in advance); early flights/family vacations are not valid excuses. Valid absences must be documented to the Registrar within 1 week of the exam; unexcused misses typically receive a zero, subject to the syllabus's stated policy.

### Article 22. Final Exam Format and Schedule

Final exams may be a final assessment, presentation, essay, coursework, or final report. Scheduled by the Registrar to avoid conflicts; the exam period follows a study period of no more than 2 weeks. A make-up final exam may be offered for valid absences, more than two exams in 24 hours, or an F on the main exam, organized within 2 weeks of the main exam's results. Students with documented learning disabilities/medical conditions may receive extra time, arranged via the Office of Student Affairs Management (student must inform the instructor in the first week of class).

### Article 23. Marking and Retaking the Final Exam

Exam questions align with course learning outcomes. Written material is stored for at least 2 years. Oral/practical exams require two instructors; disagreements are averaged, with a third instructor for large disparities. Unexcused absence from the final exam counts as one attempt with a zero score; valid, approved absences may be granted a make-up exam counted as the first attempt.

## Chapter IV. Grading Policy

### Article 24. General Principles

Instructors have sole prerogative over grades (free from bias) but must review alleged errors. Grades are reported within 7 business days after the last scheduled exam unless the Registrar grants an extension; later changes require written justification to the Registrar. Grades are not posted publicly in an identifiable way.

### Article 25. Grading System

| Grade Letter | 4-point scale |
| --- | --- |
| A | 4.0 |
| A- | 3.7 |
| B+ | 3.3 |
| B | 3.0 |
| B- | 2.7 |
| C+ | 2.3 |
| C | 2.0 |
| C- | 1.7 |
| D+ | 1.3 |
| D | 1.0 |
| D- | 0.7 |
| F | 0.0 |

Operational grades (do not count toward GPA): CR/NCR (Completed/Not Completed Requirement, for non-letter-graded required activities); I (Incomplete, must convert within 6 months or becomes F); IP (In-Progress, multi-semester projects); MT (Multi-term A/B courses); NGR (No Grade Report); W (Withdrawn); T (Transfer); Z (Exemption); AU (Audit, no grade/credit); S/U (Satisfactory/Unsatisfactory — S equates to C- or higher, U to below C-; S counts toward graduation requirements but not GPA).

### Article 26. Course Grade and GPA Calculation

Component/final exam grades use a 0–100% scale (rounded per standard rules); a total assessment score below 40% yields an F per MOET guidelines (Colleges may set stricter conditions). The final course grade combines weighted components into a 4-point/letter grade per the syllabus, fixed before the semester starts. SGPA covers all courses in one semester (including failed courses, excluding CR/NCR/IP/MT/I/NGR/S/U/W/Z), weighted by credits. CGPA covers all courses across enrollment, using only the latest attempt for repeated courses. GPA is calculated to 2 decimal digits (3rd digit rounded up). Semester GPA feeds academic distinction/scholarships/awards; Semester and Cumulative GPA feed drop-out/standing/graduation classification decisions.

### Article 27. Grade Appeal

Clerical/administrative errors are corrected by the responsible department, reported to the Registrar. Unresolved concerns may be appealed to the Registrar Office within 5 working days of the official grade posting on Canvas; see the Student Grade Appeal Procedure for details.

## Chapter V. Conferral of Awards and Graduation

### Article 28. Recognition of Graduation

Students apply for graduation in their intended graduation semester; unmet requirements require re-application. Degree conditions: minimum program credits completed; General Education/VinCore and English proficiency requirements met; major requirements completed; no unresolved "I" grades for required courses; minimum cumulative GPA of 2.00/4.00; no criminal conviction pending; other University requirements fulfilled. The Provost signs graduation recognition on the Graduation Review Board's proposal (chaired by the Provost or designee, Head of Registrar as Secretary, plus academic-unit heads and the Head of Student Affairs Management).

### Article 29. Conferment and Classification of Awards

| Classification of Awards | Cumulative GPA |
| --- | --- |
| Excellent | 3.60–4.00 |
| Very Good | 3.20–3.59 |
| Good | 2.50–3.19 |
| Marginal | 2.00–2.49 |

Excellent/Very Good awards are degraded one level if retaken (failed) courses exceed 5% of total program credits, or the student was disciplined at warning level or higher. Students who don't graduate receive a transcript of all completed/enrolled courses; students still owing National Defense Education/Physical Education certificates may complete them within 3 years post-study to become eligible for graduation.

## Chapter VI. Academic Integrity

Academic integrity is central to the VinUniversity experience; student misconduct in academic matters is not tolerated. Students pledge individually and collectively not to violate academic integrity in graded activities and to discourage others from doing so, including during professional internships (subject to host-company non-disclosure agreements). See the Student Academic Integrity policy for full detail.

## Chapter VII. Implementation

### Article 30. Implementation

This Regulation may be revised to comply with subsequent MOET regulations and applies to students enrolled in or after 2020. Faculties, Colleges, the Office of Registrar, and other relevant units are responsible for implementation. The Provost may adjust, supplement, amend, and promulgate these Regulations to align with practical implementation.
